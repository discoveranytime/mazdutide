Mazdutide
the dual-action contender

One molecule, two hormonal switches: GLP-1 to quiet appetite and glucagon to turn up energy burn. Developed with Eli Lilly and now led by Innovent in late-stage trials in China for weight management and type 2 diabetes, mazdutide is the dual agonist furthest along in the East — and still investigational everywhere. Here is what the trials actually show.

Quick read

Mazdutide (IBI362) is a dual GLP-1 and glucagon receptor agonist — it suppresses appetite like the GLP-1 drugs while adding glucagon’s energy-expenditure and liver-fat effects. In Chinese phase 3 trials (GLORY-1 for weight, DREAMS for diabetes), it delivered double-digit weight loss and strong blood-sugar control. A regulatory filing in China is underway, but mazdutide is not approved anywhere yet — this page tracks trial-design facts, not recommendations.

01The molecule

What mazdutide actually is

Mazdutide is an investigational dual agonist: one peptide that switches on both the GLP-1 (appetite) and glucagon (energy-burn) receptors. The idea: attack weight from both sides of the energy equation at once.

One chain, two locks it opens. The dual design is the whole bet: appetite down AND energy out, in a single weekly molecule.

GLP-1: the appetite switch

Like semaglutide and tirzepatide, mazdutide activates the GLP-1 receptor — quieting hunger, slowing stomach emptying, and steadying blood sugar. The proven half of the duo.

Glucagon: the burn switch

The novel half: glucagon receptor activation raises energy expenditure and targets liver fat — the reason dual agonists may out-lose single ones, in theory.

Why two beats one (maybe)

Single-agonist drugs mostly cut intake. Adding glucagon raises the other side of the equation — but also adds the side-effect and tolerability questions of a second hormone.

Plain English

Think of weight loss as a bathtub: GLP-1 turns down the faucet (appetite), glucagon opens the drain (energy burn). Single drugs only touch the faucet. Mazdutide’s bet is that working both ends drains the tub faster — if people can tolerate the double plumbing.

02The China story

The China-led path to phase 3

Mazdutide’s story runs through a Lilly–Innovent partnership and a China-first trial program — phase 3 weight and diabetes studies with a regulatory filing underway. Still investigational.

The East-led path: from partnership to phase 3.
The partnership

Mazdutide began as IBI362 in a Lilly–Innovent collaboration, then became Innovent’s lead program in China — a case study in how obesity-drug development globalized beyond the US and Europe.

GLORY-1: the weight trial

The phase 3 GLORY-1 trial tested mazdutide for weight management in Chinese adults — reporting double-digit percentage weight loss at the top dose. Trial-design fact, not a recommendation.

DREAMS: the diabetes trials

Parallel DREAMS studies tested blood-sugar control in type 2 diabetes — strong HbA1c reductions reported. Two diseases, one molecule, one regulatory path in China.

Plain English

While Western headlines tracked other dual agonists, mazdutide quietly became the dual-agonist furthest along in China — phase 3 done, filing underway. It’s a reminder that the obesity-drug race has more than one track, and this one runs through Shanghai.

03How it’s supposed to work

Two switches, one molecule

GLP-1 quiets the appetite; glucagon stokes the burn. Mazdutide pulls both levers at once — and relies on each half to cover the other’s weaknesses. Here’s the chain, as the trials measure it.

The dual-action chain — trial-measured at each step, in study populations.
The appetite half (proven)

GLP-1 effects are the known quantity: less hunger, slower gastric emptying, better post-meal sugar. Every approved drug in this family leans on them.

The burn half (the bet)

Glucagon raises energy expenditure — but glucagon also raises blood sugar on its own. The dual design counts on GLP-1 to cancel that out. Elegant on paper; tolerability is the test.

Dose reality

Phase 3 tested a multi-step dose ladder (trial-design fact, not a recommendation). Higher rungs lost more weight — and, as with all GLP-1 drugs, brought more nausea along.

Plain English

The chain is faucet + drain: eat less, burn more, liver unclogs. The clever bit is the internal truce — GLP-1 keeps glucagon’s sugar-raising mischief in check while its fat-burning does the work. Whether patients tolerate the double act long-term is what the trials are measuring.

06Risks & unknowns

Risks & unknowns

Even the most promising investigational drug carries a trial-shaped risk picture: what studies saw, what the mechanism suggests, and what nobody has measured yet.

Observed

Investigational only

Observed fact: mazdutide is not approved anywhere. Anything sold under its name today is unregulated by definition — there is no legitimate supply.

Observed

Class-typical GI effects

Observed fact: trials reported the familiar GLP-1 nausea, vomiting, and gut complaints, dose-dependent. The dual mechanism didn’t dodge the class trade-off.

Theoretical

Glucagon’s double edge

Theoretical: glucagon raises blood sugar on its own — the design relies on GLP-1 to counteract that. If the balance slips in some patients, glucose control could suffer.

Theoretical

Heart-rate and long-term CV

Theoretical: GLP-1 drugs can raise resting heart rate; adding glucagon’s metabolic push means cardiovascular outcomes need dedicated study — still pending.

Unknown

Long-term dual exposure

Unknown: years of combined GLP-1 + glucagon signaling in humans is uncharted — trials measure months to a few years.

Unknown

Beyond-trial populations

Unknown: trial volunteers are screened and monitored. Real-world use — other meds, other conditions, pregnancy — is unstudied.

Plain English

Known: investigational, with class-typical gut effects in trials. Theoretical: the glucagon balance and cardiovascular questions. Unknown: long-term dual exposure and everyone the trials screened out. A strong phase 3 doesn’t erase any of these boxes.
