Mazdutide
the dual-action contender

One molecule, two hormonal switches: GLP-1 to quiet appetite and glucagon to turn up energy burn. Developed with Eli Lilly and led by Innovent, mazdutide received its first approval in China in 2025 for long-term weight management — then for glycemic control in type 2 diabetes — while remaining investigational everywhere else. Here is what the trials actually show.

Quick read

Mazdutide (IBI362) is a once-weekly dual GLP-1 and glucagon receptor agonist — it suppresses appetite like the GLP-1 drugs while adding glucagon’s energy-expenditure and liver-fat effects. In Chinese phase 3 trials (GLORY-1 for weight, DREAMS for diabetes), it delivered double-digit weight loss and strong blood-sugar control. China’s NMPA approved it in 2025 for weight management, then for glycemic control in type 2 diabetes — outside China it remains investigational, with a U.S. phase 2 trial reporting in 2026. This page tracks trial-design facts, not recommendations.

01The molecule

What mazdutide actually is

Mazdutide is a dual agonist: one peptide that switches on both the GLP-1 (appetite) and glucagon (energy-burn) receptors. The idea: attack weight from both sides of the energy equation at once. China’s NMPA approved it in 2025 for long-term weight management — then for glycemic control in type 2 diabetes — while it remains investigational elsewhere.

One chain, two locks it opens. The dual design is the whole bet: appetite down AND energy out, in a single weekly molecule.

GLP-1: the appetite switch

Like semaglutide and tirzepatide, mazdutide activates the GLP-1 receptor — quieting hunger, slowing stomach emptying, and steadying blood sugar. The proven half of the duo.

Glucagon: the burn switch

The novel half: glucagon receptor activation raises energy expenditure and targets liver fat — the reason dual agonists may out-lose single ones, in theory.

Why two beats one (maybe)

Single-agonist drugs mostly cut intake. Adding glucagon raises the other side of the equation — but also adds the side-effect and tolerability questions of a second hormone.

Regulatory status

First approval: China (NMPA), 2025 — long-term weight management, then glycemic control in type 2 diabetes. Investigational everywhere else — including the U.S., where a phase 2 trial reported in 2026. The investigational-stage research that built this page is now a pre-approval history, not the current status.

Plain English

Think of weight loss as a bathtub: GLP-1 turns down the faucet (appetite), glucagon opens the drain (energy burn). Single drugs only touch the faucet. Mazdutide’s bet is that working both ends drains the tub faster — if people can tolerate the double plumbing.

02The China story

The China-led path to phase 3

Mazdutide’s story runs through a Lilly–Innovent partnership and a China-first trial program — phase 3 weight and diabetes studies that led to China’s NMPA approval in 2025, with U.S. testing still underway.

The East-led path: from partnership to China approval in 2025.
The partnership

Mazdutide began as IBI362 in a Lilly–Innovent collaboration, then became Innovent’s lead program in China — a case study in how obesity-drug development globalized beyond the US and Europe. A 2026 U.S. phase 2 trial (48 weeks) is now testing it in a Western population.

GLORY-1: the weight trial

The phase 3 GLORY-1 trial tested mazdutide for weight management in Chinese adults — reporting double-digit percentage weight loss at the top dose. That data underpinned the 2025 NMPA approval for weight management. Trial-design fact, not a recommendation.

DREAMS: the diabetes trials

Parallel DREAMS studies tested blood-sugar control in type 2 diabetes — strong HbA1c reductions reported. The diabetes indication followed the weight-management approval in China. Two diseases, one molecule, one regulatory path.

Plain English

While Western headlines tracked other dual agonists, mazdutide quietly became the first dual GLP-1/glucagon agonist approved anywhere — China’s NMPA cleared it for weight management in 2025, then for type 2 diabetes glycemic control. It’s a reminder that the obesity-drug race has more than one track, and this one runs through Shanghai. Outside China, it remains investigational.

03How it’s supposed to work

Two switches, one molecule

GLP-1 quiets the appetite; glucagon stokes the burn. Mazdutide pulls both levers at once — and relies on each half to cover the other’s weaknesses. The receptors sit on cell membranes; mazdutide binds from outside and never enters cells. Here is the biology, tissue by tissue.

The GLP-1 half: after a meal the gut releases GLP-1; mazdutide docks at the same GLP-1 receptor — from outside the cell. The receptor, not the drug, tells each tissue what to do.
The glucagon half: one mazdutide molecule also fits the glucagon receptor on liver cells — again binding from outside. The burn rises; the sugar-raising side of glucagon is why the GLP-1 half is in the design.
Two partially opposing forces — one combined outcome. Glucagon does not simply “burn fat”; weight changes through a sustained energy deficit, gradually, over weeks.
The appetite half (proven)

GLP-1 effects are the known quantity: less hunger, slower gastric emptying, better post-meal sugar. Every approved drug in this family leans on them.

The burn half (the bet)

Glucagon raises energy expenditure — but glucagon also raises blood sugar on its own. The dual design counts on GLP-1 to cancel that out. Elegant on paper; tolerability is the test.

Dose-response, not a dosing guide

Trials tested multiple dose levels — higher levels trended toward more weight loss and more GI effects. That pattern is a trial-design fact; it is not instructions for anyone to follow.

Why dual, not just “stronger Ozempic”

Mazdutide is not a bigger GLP-1 hammer — it adds a second hormonal signal with different physiology. The two forces partially oppose each other (glucagon raises glucose; GLP-1 steadies it) and the design bets their combined balance beats either alone. More receptors does not automatically mean more potent — it means a different metabolic profile.

Plain English

The chain is faucet + drain: eat less, burn more, liver unclogs. The clever bit is the internal truce — GLP-1 keeps glucagon’s sugar-raising mischief in check while the burn does the work. Whether people tolerate the double act long-term is what the trials measure — and what the 2025 China approval and the ongoing U.S. phase 2 are answering in stages.

06A vs B

Mazdutide vs the field

Four drugs, four receptor recipes. The receptor profile — not the number of receptors — is what gives each drug its metabolic personality. More switches does not automatically mean more potent.

Four receptor recipes. The combination — not the count — decides what each drug’s metabolism looks like.

Semaglutide — the single

GLP-1 only. The proven baseline: appetite down, sugar steadier, years of safety data. Every dual and triple agonist is measured against it.

Tirzepatide — the other dual

GLP-1 + GIP. A different second signal: GIP supports glucose control and may soften GI effects. Dual — but a different dual than mazdutide.

Mazdutide — this page

GLP-1 + glucagon. The only dual of the four to bet on energy expenditure instead of GIP. Approved in China (2025); investigational elsewhere.

Retatrutide — the triple

GLP-1 + GIP + glucagon. Both second signals at once. Shares glucagon activity with mazdutide — but adds GIP and remains investigational (BLA expected 2027). Not the same drug, not the same profile.

Mazdutide vs retatrutide — don’t confuse them

Both hit the glucagon receptor, which is why they get mentioned together. But retatrutide adds GIP (mazdutide doesn’t), sits at a different development stage, and the two have never been compared head-to-head in a trial. Shared glucagon activity ≠ interchangeable drugs.

Why receptor combos give different profiles

GLP-1 quiets appetite; GIP tunes glucose handling and tolerability; glucagon pushes energy expenditure and raises glucose. Each combination therefore lands differently on weight, sugar, liver fat, and side effects — the recipe shapes the result, not the receptor count.

More receptors ≠ automatically stronger

Three switches is not “50% better than two.” Each added signal brings its own side-effect ledger (glucagon’s glucose push, GI burden) — potency is measured in trials, never assumed from the diagram.

Plain English

Think of these as four different engine tunes: same chassis, different power delivery. Semaglutide is the reliable single; tirzepatide adds GIP’s smoothness; mazdutide swaps GIP for glucagon’s burn; retatrutide runs all three. The tune you want depends on the whole metabolic profile — not how many switches are on the dashboard.

07Potential benefits

Potential benefits, by evidence tier

Mazdutide’s benefits live on three very different evidence tiers — trial facts, emerging signals, and community stories. They are not equal, so they don’t share a shelf.

Tier 1 · Established

What the trials demonstrated

  • Weight loss in phase 3 — GLORY-1 reported double-digit percentage loss vs placebo, the outcome behind the 2025 NMPA weight-management approval in China.
  • Blood-sugar control in phase 3 — the DREAMS program reported strong HbA1c reductions in type 2 diabetes, the outcome behind the glycemic-control approval.
  • The two-lever bet paid off in trials — study populations showed both halves working: GLP-1 appetite suppression plus glucagon-driven energy expenditure.

Think of it this way: a trial result is a class report card — the class average was strong enough to win an approval, but no single student’s grade is guaranteed.

Tier 2 · Predicted or emerging

Signals that need more proof — explicitly labeled

  • Liver-fat reduction — secondary-endpoint signals point the right way, but liver endpoints were not the trials’ primary targets. Emerging, not established.
  • Cardiovascular outcomes — still pending. GLP-1 drugs can raise resting heart rate and glucagon adds a metabolic push, so dedicated CV studies are needed before any heart claim.
  • U.S. phase 2 (2026) — a 48-week trial extending the China results into a Western population. A data gate, not a result.
  • Long-term maintenance — trials measure months to a few years; years of dual exposure remain uncharted.
Tier 3 · Community anecdotes

Stories, not data — kept far from the trial evidence

  • The mechanism fans — “finally, a drug that burns while it quiets.” Enthusiasm for the idea, not evidence for the outcome.
  • The liver-fat hopefuls — “I care less about the scale than my fatty liver numbers.” A reasonable hope; forum labs are unverifiable.
  • The tolerability logs — week-three nausea “like the early semaglutide days.” Scattered self-reports; drug, dose, and identity unverifiable.
  • The plateau reports and the quiet ones — stalls after fast early loss, and non-responders who “barely noticed anything.” The underreported null result.

Established: it moved the scale and the sugar numbers in phase 3 — that’s why a regulator approved it. Emerging: the liver, heart, and long-term questions are still open files. Anecdotal: the forums are enthusiastic, queasy, and honest about plateaus — interesting, not evidence.

08Fit check

Who it may suit — and who it should not

One molecule, two very different answers depending on where you stand. Established medical indications first — then the honest boundaries.

✓ Who it may be relevant to

  • Adults with obesity or overweight, as studied in GLORY-1 — the population behind China’s weight-management approval.
  • Adults with type 2 diabetes, as studied in DREAMS — the population behind China’s glycemic-control approval.
  • People in China, where mazdutide is an approved prescription medicine used under medical supervision.
  • Clinicians and researchers tracking the dual-agonist class as the evidence develops.

✕ Who it may not suit — extra caution

  • Anyone outside China — investigational there, with no legitimate supply; anything sold under its name is unregulated by definition.

  • Pregnancy, breastfeeding, and trial-screened-out groups — unstudied in the trial programs.
  • Anyone considering gray-market “mazdutide” — no quality oversight and unknown contents.

09Risks & unknowns

Risks & unknowns

Even a newly approved drug carries a trial-shaped risk picture: what studies saw, what the mechanism suggests, and what nobody has measured yet.

Observed

China-only approval

Observed fact: mazdutide is approved in China (2025) and investigational everywhere else. Anything sold under its name outside China is unregulated by definition — there is no legitimate supply there.

Observed

Class-typical GI effects

Observed fact: trials reported the familiar GLP-1 nausea, vomiting, and gut complaints, dose-dependent. The dual mechanism didn’t dodge the class trade-off.

Theoretical

Glucagon’s double edge

Theoretical: glucagon raises blood sugar on its own — the design relies on GLP-1 to counteract that. If the balance slips in some patients, glucose control could suffer.

Theoretical

Heart-rate and long-term CV

Theoretical: GLP-1 drugs can raise resting heart rate; adding glucagon’s metabolic push means cardiovascular outcomes need dedicated study — still pending.

Unknown

Long-term dual exposure

Unknown: years of combined GLP-1 + glucagon signaling in humans is uncharted — trials measure months to a few years.

Unknown

Beyond-trial populations

Unknown: trial volunteers are screened and monitored. Real-world use — other meds, other conditions, pregnancy — is unstudied.

Plain English

Known: investigational, with class-typical gut effects in trials. Theoretical: the glucagon balance and cardiovascular questions. Unknown: long-term dual exposure and everyone the trials screened out. A strong phase 3 doesn’t erase any of these boxes.

Mazdutide activates GLP-1 (appetite down) and glucagon (burn up) — the dual-agonist bet.

🇨🇳

China-led to phase 3

A Lilly–Innovent origin, then Innovent’s GLORY-1 (weight) and DREAMS (diabetes) programs in China.

📉

Strong trial signals

Reported double-digit weight loss and strong HbA1c drops — real phase 3 human data.

✅

Approved in China, 2025

NMPA cleared it for weight management, then glycemic control in type 2 diabetes. Investigational everywhere else — the U.S. phase 2 reports in 2026.

🍽️

Faucet + drain

GLP-1 cuts intake; glucagon raises burn and targets liver fat — both sides of the equation.

⚖️

The internal truce

GLP-1 is counted on to cancel glucagon’s sugar-raising — elegant design, tolerability is the test.

🤢

Class-typical gut effects

Trials showed the familiar nausea-heavy GLP-1 trade-off, dose-dependent.

❤️

Heart questions open

Long-term cardiovascular data for dual agonism are still pending — the key unknown.

💬

Watch the data, not the hype

Community chatter is stories, not data — and even strong phase 3 was only the start of the regulatory road.

🔭

What would change the story

Western trial readouts and filings — then the harder question of global access.
